Tabs across the top, a clickable breadcrumb path, the familiar sidebar — and the view flips through Explorer's eight modes, here from large icons to a detailed list. Everything where a Windows hand expects it.

Windows 11 habits, native on the Mac

The shortcuts and layout you already have in your fingers — running as a real macOS app.

Browser-style tabs

Open as many folders as you like in one window. New tab with ⌘T, close with ⌘W or the middle mouse button, switch with Ctrl+Tab or ⌘1–9. Drag tabs to reorder, tear one out into its own window, or pick from the Chrome-style dropdown of all open tabs. They come back when you relaunch.

All eight view modes

Extra-large, large, medium and small icons, List, Details, Tiles and Content — the full Win11 set, switchable from the View menu, the folder's right-click menu, or by holding Ctrl/⌘ and scrolling the wheel. A compact-spacing toggle tightens the rows when you want more on screen.

Win11 shortcuts, dual-bound

Copy, cut, paste, undo/redo, select-all, rename, new folder — every file shortcut answers to both the Ctrl combo from Windows and the ⌘ combo a Mac expects. F2 renames, F5 refreshes, Del sends to the Recycle Bin, Shift+Del skips it. Keep your muscle memory; the key bindings adapt.

Real QuickLook previews

Thumbnails come from macOS's own QuickLook engine — true previews of images, video and PDFs in the larger icon modes, generated lazily only for what's on screen and cached so scrolling stays smooth. The familiar look of Windows, with the Mac's native preview quality underneath.

A breadcrumb address bar

Every segment of the path is clickable, and each has a dropdown of its siblings for one-hop jumps. Click the empty space and it turns into an editable path field — type an absolute path or ~ with autocomplete, or pull from history. Back, Forward, Up and Backspace all behave the Windows way.
